# A Modular Ordinal Analysis via Reflection

This note combines Beklemishev’s worms, viewed as finite representations of iterated reflection<Citation number="1" source="beklemishev2004" />, with transfinite iterations of reflection indexed by Girard’s dilators<Citation number="2" source="girard1981" />. Its goal is to describe conservation spectra for theories of second-order arithmetic. The text is a web-oriented reconstruction of the research draft *modular ordinal analysis via reflection*.

We work mainly over $\mathsf{ACA}_0$, following Simpson<Citation number="3" source="simpson2009" /> for the standard subsystems of second-order arithmetic and their $\omega$-models. The notation $\Pi^1_k\text{-}\omega\mathsf{RFN}(T)$ denotes $\Pi^1_k$ $\omega$-model reflection over a theory $T$.

## 1. Worms and ordinal assignments

<MathStatement kind="definition" number="1.1" title="Worms">

For $n\in\omega$, define the set $\mathbb W_n$ of worms inductively:

1. $\top\in\mathbb W_n$.
2. If $m\ge n$ and $w\in\mathbb W_n$, then $\langle m\rangle w\in\mathbb W_n$.

Define a relation $\prec_n$ on $\mathbb W_n$ by

$$
w\prec_n w'
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathsf{RC}\vdash w'\rightarrow\langle n\rangle w.
$$

For $m\ge n$, put

$$
\langle m\rangle\mathbb W_n
:=
\{\langle m\rangle w:w\in\mathbb W_n\}\cup\{\top\}.
$$

</MathStatement>

Here $\mathsf{RC}$ is the worm calculus governing the interaction of the modalities $\langle n\rangle$<Citation number="1" source="beklemishev2004" />. We write $\equiv_{\mathsf{RC}}$ for mutual derivability in this calculus.

<MathStatement kind="definition" number="1.2" title="Ordinal assignment">

Define $o=o_0:\mathbb W_0\to\varepsilon_0$ along Cantor normal forms. First,

$$
o(\langle0\rangle^k\top)=k.
$$

For $w\in\mathbb W_1$, define $w\mapsto w^-$ by

$$
\top^-:=\top,
\qquad
(\langle m\rangle w)^-:=\langle m-1\rangle w^-
\quad(m\ge1).
$$

For $w_0,\ldots,w_{k-1}\in\mathbb W_1$, let

$$
o(w_0\langle0\rangle w_1\langle0\rangle\cdots\langle0\rangle w_{k-1})
=
\omega^{o(w_{k-1}^-)}+\cdots+\omega^{o(w_0^-)}.
$$

The general assignment $o_n:\mathbb W_n\to\varepsilon_0$ is obtained by shifting all modality indices down by $n$.

</MathStatement>

<MathStatement kind="proposition" number="1.3" title="Ignatiev">

The map $o_n:\mathbb W_n\to\varepsilon_0$ is surjective and strictly order-preserving, and it induces an order isomorphism

$$
\mathbb W_n/{\equiv_{\mathsf{RC}}}\cong\varepsilon_0.
$$

Ignatiev normal forms $w^{\mathsf{NF}}$ provide representatives of the equivalence classes<Citation number="4" source="ignatiev1993" />.

</MathStatement>

<MathStatement kind="definition" number="1.4" title="Ω-assignment">

Define the base change $S^\omega_\Omega:\varepsilon_0\to\mathsf{PDil}$ by

$$
S^\omega_\Omega(0)=0
$$

and

$$
S^\omega_\Omega
(\omega^{\xi_{k-1}}+\cdots+\omega^{\xi_0})
=
\Omega^{S^\omega_\Omega(\xi_{k-1})}+\cdots+
\Omega^{S^\omega_\Omega(\xi_0)}.
$$

Here $\Omega$ is the identity praedilator. Put

$$
o_n^\Omega:=S^\omega_\Omega\circ o_n.
$$

Thus the base $\omega$ in the ordinary ordinal assignment is replaced by the dilator variable $\Omega$. See Girard<Citation number="2" source="girard1981" /> for the definition and basic properties of dilators.

</MathStatement>

## 2. Interpreting worms by reflection

<MathStatement kind="definition" number="2.1" title="Interpretation by ω-model reflection">

Let $T\supseteq\mathsf{ACA}_0$ be a theory of second-order arithmetic. Define the interpretation $(w)^{\omega\mathsf{RFN}}_T$ of a worm $w\in\mathbb W_0$ by

$$
(\top)^{\omega\mathsf{RFN}}_T:=\top
$$

and

$$
(\langle m\rangle w)^{\omega\mathsf{RFN}}_T
:=
\Pi^1_{m+2}\text{-}\omega\mathsf{RFN}
\bigl(T+(w)^{\omega\mathsf{RFN}}_T\bigr).
$$

The modality $\langle m\rangle$ is therefore read as one application of $\Pi^1_{m+2}$ $\omega$-model reflection.

</MathStatement>

This interpretation transfers the combinatorics of worms to iterations of reflection. For example, $\langle1\rangle\top$ represents $\Pi^1_3$ $\omega$-model reflection, while $\langle0\rangle\langle1\rangle\top$ adds $\Pi^1_2$ $\omega$-model reflection on top of it.

## 3. Iterating reflection with dilators

The following dilator-indexed iteration of reflection adapts a construction presented in a lecture by Pakhomov<Citation number="5" source="pakhomov2023" /> to the notation of the research manuscript. The presently available source is a seminar record, rather than a refereed publication.

<MathStatement kind="definition" number="3.1" title="Shift">

Let $\langle D;\operatorname{supp}^D\rangle$ be a praedilator and $\mathcal A$ a linear order. Define the shift $D[\mathcal A]$ by

$$
D[\mathcal A](\mathcal X):=D(\mathcal A+\mathcal X),
\qquad
D[\mathcal A](f):=D(\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal A}+f),
$$

with support

$$
\operatorname{supp}^{D[\mathcal A]}_{\mathcal X}(\sigma)
:=
\operatorname{supp}^D_{\mathcal A+\mathcal X}(\sigma)
\mathbin{\upharpoonright}\mathcal X.
$$

</MathStatement>

<MathStatement kind="proposition" number="3.2">

Over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, shifting a dilator by a well-order yields another dilator<Citation number="2" source="girard1981" />.

</MathStatement>

<MathStatement kind="definition" number="3.3" title="Trace and constants">

The trace of a praedilator $D$ is

$$
\operatorname{Tr}(D)
:=
\{(n,\sigma):n\in\omega,\ \sigma\in D(n),\ 
\operatorname{supp}^D_n(\sigma)=n\}.
$$

An element $(0,\sigma)\in\operatorname{Tr}(D)$ is called a **constant** of $D$. The set of constants is denoted by $\operatorname{Tr}_0(D)$.

</MathStatement>

<MathStatement kind="definition" number="3.4" title="Restriction at a constant">

For $c\in\operatorname{Tr}_0(D)$, define $D\mathbin{\upharpoonright}c$ by

$$
(D\mathbin{\upharpoonright}c)(\mathcal X)
:=
D(\mathcal X)\mathbin{\upharpoonright}c.
$$

Its action on embeddings and its support transformation are obtained by restricting those of $D$ to this initial segment.

</MathStatement>

<MathStatement kind="definition" number="3.5" title="Dilator-indexed reflection">

Let $T$ be a theory with an elementary recursive axiomatization, and let $\mathsf{RFN}$ be a reflection principle represented by a single formula. By the fixed-point lemma, define $\mathsf{RFN}^D(T)$ through

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{RCA}_0\vdash\quad
\mathsf{RFN}^D(T)\ \longleftrightarrow\ 
&\mathsf{PDil}(D)\ \land\\
&(\forall\mathcal X)\Bigl[
\mathsf{WO}(\mathcal X)\to
(\forall c\in\operatorname{Tr}_0(D[\mathcal X]))\\
&\hspace{4em}\mathsf{RFN}
\bigl(T+\mathsf{RFN}^{D[\mathcal X]\upharpoonright c}(T)\bigr)
\Bigr].
\end{aligned}
$$

Also put

$$
\mathsf R^D(T)
:=
T+\{\mathsf{RFN}^{D[1]\upharpoonright c}(T):
c\in\operatorname{Tr}_0(D[1])\}.
$$

</MathStatement>

<MathStatement kind="remark" number="3.6" title="Formalization">

A complete formalization over the base theory requires coded praedilators<Citation number="6" source="freund2024" />. This note suppresses those coding details in order to make the structure of the conservation spectrum visible.

</MathStatement>

## 4. Conservation spectra

Write $\alpha\llbracket k\rrbracket$ for the $k$-th member of the fundamental sequence for $\alpha$, and $U\equiv_\Gamma V$ when $U$ and $V$ have the same consequences in the class $\Gamma$.

### Notation at a glance

| Notation | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| $\Pi^1_r\text{-}\omega\mathsf{RFN}(T)$ | $\Pi^1_r$ $\omega$-model reflection over $T$ |
| $\Pi^1_r\text{-}\omega\mathsf{RFN}^{D}(T)$ | $\Pi^1_r$ $\omega$-model reflection iterated along a dilator $D$ |
| $(w)^{\omega\mathsf{RFN}}_T$ | the sentence obtained by interpreting the worm $w$ as iterated reflection over $T$ |
| $o_n(w)$ | the ordinary ordinal assignment of a worm $w\in\mathbb W_n$ |
| $o_n^\Omega(w)=S^\omega_\Omega(o_n(w))$ | the assignment obtained by changing the base $\omega$ to the dilator variable $\Omega$ |
| $U\equiv_\Gamma V$ | $U$ and $V$ have the same consequences in the class $\Gamma$ |
| $\alpha\llbracket k\rrbracket$ | the $k$-th member of the fundamental sequence for $\alpha$ |

The next theorem is not reproduced verbatim from an external source. It is the main result of the research manuscript, combining the frameworks of References <Citation number="1" source="beklemishev2004" /><Citation number="5" source="pakhomov2023" />. A complete proof is not yet included in this web version.

<MathStatement kind="theorem" number="4.1" title="Conservation spectrum theorem">

Let $n\in\omega$ and $m\ge n$. Suppose that $T$ is a $\Pi^1_{n+2}$ extension of $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ with an elementary recursive axiomatization.

1. If $w\in\langle m\rangle\mathbb W_m$, then

   $$
   T+(w)^{\omega\mathsf{RFN}}_T
   \equiv
   T+\Pi^1_{m+2}\text{-}\omega\mathsf{RFN}^{\langle m\rangle o_m^\Omega(w)}(T).
   $$

2. If $w\in\mathbb W_m\setminus\langle m\rangle\mathbb W_m$, then

   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   T+(w)^{\omega\mathsf{RFN}}_T
   \equiv_{\Pi^1_{m+2}}
   T+\{&\Pi^1_{m+2}\text{-}\omega\mathsf{RFN}^{
   S^\omega_\Omega(o_m(w)\llbracket k\rrbracket)}(T):\\
   &k\in\omega\}.
   \end{aligned}
   $$

</MathStatement>

In the first case, the worm assignment gives a single dilator-indexed iteration. In the second, its fundamental sequence yields a family of reflection principles, which is the conservation spectrum for the weaker formula class.

## 5. Calculations

<MathStatement kind="example" number="5.1" title="Σ¹₁ dependent choice">

The research manuscript takes the following reflection-theoretic characterization as its starting point:

$$
\Sigma^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{DC}_0
\equiv
\mathsf{ACA}_0+(\langle1\rangle\top)^{\omega\mathsf{RFN}}_{\mathsf{ACA}_0}.
$$

Since $o_0(\langle1\rangle\top)=\omega$ and $\omega\llbracket k\rrbracket=k$, we obtain

$$
\Sigma^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{DC}_0
\equiv_{\Pi^1_2}
\mathsf{ACA}_0+
\{\Pi^1_2\text{-}\omega\mathsf{RFN}^{k}(\mathsf{ACA}_0):k\in\omega\}.
$$

</MathStatement>

<MathStatement kind="example" number="5.2" title="Arithmetical transfinite recursion">

Under the reflection-theoretic characterization used in the manuscript, $\mathsf{ATR}_0$ is represented by the worm $\langle0\rangle\langle1\rangle\top$. Its ordinary assignment is $\omega+1$; after the relevant collapse and base change, the value becomes $\Omega$. Hence

$$
\mathsf{ATR}_0
\equiv
\mathsf{ACA}_0+
\Pi^1_2\text{-}\omega\mathsf{RFN}^{\Omega}(\mathsf{ACA}_0).
$$

Equivalently, this has the form

$$
\mathsf{ACA}_0+
(\forall\xi)\bigl[
\mathsf{WO}(\xi)\to
\Pi^1_2\text{-}\omega\mathsf{RFN}^{\xi}(\mathsf{ACA}_0)
\bigr].
$$

</MathStatement>

The last column must be read with a distinction between full equivalence and conservation expressed by $\equiv_{\Pi^1_r}$. Rows written as families in braces are cases where a fundamental sequence of reflection principles gives the conservative approximation, rather than a single limit-stage principle.

The connection between $\Sigma^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{TDC}_0$ and the existence of hyperations for WPO-dilators is attributed to Provenzano’s unpublished master’s thesis<Citation number="10" source="provenzano2022" />. Because this is not a public refereed source, it is treated here only as supplementary information.
